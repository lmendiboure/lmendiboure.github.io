“Monitoring is no longer enough. The system may increase ventilation, but the operator must retain authority — and a controller can acknowledge a command digitally even if the physical actuator is jammed.”
DESIGN IMPACTThe design must distinguish a command, digital evidence and evidence of the physical result.
FOLLOW ONE CLAIM AT A TIME

What does the system actually know after it sends a command?

Keep the monitoring design. Follow just one ventilation command. Each step asks what the available evidence really proves; the control-loop model appears only after those distinctions have been made.

Incident trace

CO₂ is high. The service commands ventilation to 60%.

The controller returns a digital acknowledgement (ACK): it confirms message receipt or handling, not the physical result. The damper may still be mechanically jammed. A human operator must be able to supersede automation.
