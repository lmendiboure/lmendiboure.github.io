Activity 8Choose the delivery semantics

“Reliable” is not one guarantee.

Different services tolerate different losses, duplicates and disconnections. Commit to the guarantee each case actually needs, then place concrete mechanisms at the responsibility that provides them.

Mechanism map

Who can provide which part of the guarantee?

Drag or tap a mechanism, then place it at the responsibility where it primarily acts. Several layers may cooperate end-to-end.

STOP
Class discussion

Guarantees belong to a scope.

BoundaryWhen someone says “exactly once”, exactly once what — a message, an application operation, or a physical effect?
KEEP

Transport and application protocols can improve delivery behaviour, but end-to-end correctness may still require application idempotence, deduplication, persistence, freshness checks and physical-state feedback.

Activity 9Stress-test the exchange

Which guarantee actually failed?

Choose one incident. Name the guarantee that should have held, identify the responsible mechanism(s), then revise the exchange. A lower layer may be behaving correctly while the service still fails.

Δ
Session 2 complete

Keep the composition rule.

A protocol name is not the architecture, delivered bytes are not shared meaning, and “reliable” only makes sense once its scope is explicit. Build the exchange from service semantics to end-to-end guarantees.
