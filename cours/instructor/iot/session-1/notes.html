01

Test the IoT boundary

Low floor: create a useful shared systems boundary.

5 min work · 5 min discussion
Why this now

Students need a common object of study before designing the campus case.

Students should leave with

Connected alone is insufficient; trace the physical-world role and the digital service.

Watch for

Do not let domain classification become the task. The domain tags are scaffolding.

Transition

“If these systems share a skeleton, what responsibilities and flows does our campus service actually need?”

02

Build a defensible baseline

Turn service facts into responsibilities and labelled information flows.

8 min work · 6 min discussion
Why this now

The class needs an architecture artefact that later requirements and technology choices can refer back to.

Students should leave with

At least three responsibilities/components, two labelled flows, and one unresolved assumption.

Watch for

Product/protocol names used as responsibilities; unlabeled arrows; assumptions silently treated as facts.

Transition

“Monitoring only observes. What changes in the architecture once the system can act on the physical world?”

03

From monitoring to control

Separate digital acknowledgement from physical-state evidence.

6 min work · 6 min discussion
Why this now

Actuation creates new flows and claims; this is the cleanest place to introduce feedback and authority.

Students should leave with

Command sent ≠ accepted ≠ physical action performed. Feedback and override authority must be explicit.

Watch for

An ACK presented as proof of physical success; human authority left outside the architecture.

Transition

“Now that flows have different roles, do they really have the same communication requirements?”

04

Expose per-flow communication requirements

Turn vague needs into decision-relevant constraints.

6 min work · 5 min discussion
Why this now

A technology comparison without explicit requirements is mostly familiarity bias.

Students should leave with

A system Top 3, a Top 3 for one concrete flow, and an explanation of any difference.

Watch for

Latency confused with throughput; “reliability” used without saying what must remain available.

Transition

“We finally know what communication must achieve. What network structures could satisfy that before we name a standard?”

Reference vocabulary
Range · payload/data volume · throughput · latency · reliability/availability · energy · scale · mobility · infrastructure · deployment/maintenance · cost. Security, privacy, manageability and safety cut across these dimensions.
05

From network shape to technology family

One two-step activity: structure first, names second.

≈12 min work · no separate restitution
Why this now

This is the restart point for the next class. Use the Projector recap to give the previous learning directly, then use the established requirements to compare network shapes before revealing technology names.

Students should leave with

Leading network shape + challenger + decisive known fact + missing fact; then the hidden infrastructure/stack commitments behind the linked technology families.

Watch for

Students jumping straight to Wi‑Fi/LoRaWAN; fixed-range memorisation; treating IEEE 802.15.4, LoRaWAN and cellular IoT as identical abstraction levels.

Transition

“A candidate can fit today and fail tomorrow if the assumption carrying the choice changes. Test that before committing the campus strategy.”

Technical points worth having ready
BLE: nearby low-energy peer/gateway. Wi‑Fi: AP + IP network. 802.15.4: local PHY/MAC building block; upper layers required. LoRaWAN: device → gateway → network service. NB-IoT/LTE-M: base station → operator network. Satellite/NTN is deliberately deferred to S4 when terrestrial coverage failure gives it an architectural reason to appear.
06

Defend the campus communication strategy

Test one technology fit, break its assumption, then return to campus.

8 min work · 8 min discussion
Why this now

The session should end with a conditional engineering decision, not a technology catalogue.

Students should leave with

One transfer decision + decisive assumption + counterfactual; then one campus stance and the missing evidence that could reverse it.

Watch for

“One family everywhere” treated as an engineering requirement; “not enough information” without naming what information is missing.

Transition

Close S1 on: service → flows → requirements → network shape → technology family → validity domain. S2 assumes the path exists and asks how messages actually move through it.
