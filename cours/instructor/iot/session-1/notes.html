Non-negotiable outcomes

  • Students can distinguish system responsibilities/flows from product names.
  • Students can derive communication requirements before selecting technologies.
  • Students can explain one architecture revision as changed assumption → changed requirement → changed architecture.

01
Activity 1 of 8

Map the IoT landscape

Use classification disagreement to reveal a cross-domain systems field.

6–7 min work · 6–7 min restitutionCore cap · 10 min
Operational trigger to stage

Amina questions whether the “IoT” label is a useful boundary when it is applied to very different connected physical systems. Let the mapping activity generate the commonalities; do not state them in the trigger.

Watch for
  • Different domains for the same case.
  • Recurring physical-to-digital structure.
Validate

Several domain labels can be defensible; domain classification is not the learning target.

Correct if needed
  • Do not define IoT as “anything connected”.
  • Do not claim one universal literature definition.
Landing point / transition

Land on the working definition, then extend beyond domains to common IoT system purposes.

Reference plate / step further

Know: monitoring, metering, tracking, remote control, closed-loop automation, alerting/safety, asset management, predictive maintenance, optimisation/coordination.

Build architecture v1

Separate responsibilities, components and information flows.

10–12 min work · 7–8 min restitutionCore cap · 14 min
Operational trigger to stage

Amina hands over Case file 01. Do not reread the dossier through her dialogue: her function is to force students to distinguish evidence from assumptions before committing Architecture v1.

Watch for
  • Mixtures of function, physical box and product.
  • Missing users, storage, alert paths or dependencies.
Validate

Different decompositions are valid if responsibilities and flows are explainable.

Correct if needed
  • Cloud, MQTT, LoRaWAN or Raspberry Pi are not architectural responsibilities.
  • Five roles are a lens, not five mandatory boxes.
Landing point / transition

Use a few contrasting student architectures; then give the wider architecture reference map.

03
Activity 3 of 8

Close the loop

Expose the difference between observing and acting on the physical world.

7–9 min work · 6–7 min restitutionCore cap · 12 min
Operational trigger to stage

Amina brings an amended operating contract: observation/history/alerts remain in scope, but ventilation may now be controlled under operator authority. The student surface deliberately shows the closed-loop chain up front. Do not make them rediscover boxes by clicking; use the activity to force three boundary decisions: physical evidence, ACK scope and human authority.

Watch for
  • Whether groups distinguish command acknowledgement from physical state feedback.
  • Whether the three visible dimensions are covered: action path, outcome evidence, authority/resilience.
  • No written rationale is required: the reasoning is defended orally at the STOP.
Validate

A defensible loop contains observation, decision, command/actuation and feedback; authority must be explicit.

Correct if needed
  • Command sent ≠ command accepted ≠ physical action performed.
  • A network ACK is not necessarily evidence of physical success.
Landing point / transition

Use the jammed-actuator counterexample, then connect closed-loop flows to later communication requirements.

Reference plate / step further

Four distinct flows/claims: telemetry · command · acknowledgement · state feedback. Also make human override / policy and safe/default behaviour explicit.

04
Activity 4 of 8

Expose communication requirements

Turn vague needs into per-flow engineering constraints.

6–8 min work · 6–7 min restitutionCore cap · 11 min
Operational trigger to stage

Sam brings new field evidence that makes the existing communication arrows non-equivalent. The evidence changes the decision; it should not restate the original campus brief.

Watch for
  • Different Top 3s due to different assumptions.
  • “Fast” used ambiguously.
Validate

Requirements should attach to a flow/service and explicit assumptions.

Correct if needed
  • Latency ≠ throughput.
  • Reliability ≠ always connected.
Landing point / transition

Land on per-flow requirements before comparing technologies.

Reference plate / step further

Reference map: range · payload/data volume · throughput · latency · reliability/availability · energy · scale · mobility · infrastructure · deployment/maintenance · cost. Cross-cutting: security · privacy · manageability · safety for actuation.

Activity 5 of 8

Discover network shapes

Compare network shapes from the communication problem before committing a technology choice.

6–7 min work · no restitutionCore cap · 5 min
Operational trigger to stage

Yanis receives the requirement evidence but no site-level coverage answer yet. Keep all five network shapes visible, but stage the task as an assumption test: one leading campus hypothesis, one challenger, the fact currently carrying the ranking, and the missing observation most likely to reverse it.

Watch for
  • Students should read across distance, traffic, device budget and infrastructure, then distinguish current evidence from the unresolved site fact that could falsify their ranking.
Validate

Network shapes expose different peer relationships, ownership boundaries and infrastructure dependencies.

Correct if needed

Do not interrupt with whole-class correction; protect the detective in Activity 6.

Landing point / transition

Continue directly.

06
Activity 6 of 8

Investigate technologies

Attach technology names and scope to the previously built network-shape map.

9–11 min work · no restitutionCore cap · 7 min
Operational trigger to stage

The integrator shortlist arrives with Wi-Fi, Bluetooth LE, IEEE 802.15.4, LoRaWAN and NB-IoT but almost no infrastructure or architectural commitments. The missing commitments, not the names themselves, create the need for investigation.

Watch for
  • LoRa vs LoRaWAN, 802.15.4 scope, private LoRaWAN, cellular/operator dependencies.
  • All evidence is visible from the start; students now only choose → commit. The reference drawer is support, not a second workflow.
Validate

The goal is not memorising fixed ranges; it is understanding structures, scope and trade-offs.

Correct if needed
  • 802.15.4 is PHY/MAC, not a complete IoT stack.
  • Do not introduce Satellite/NTN as a sixth equivalent family here. Reserve it for Activity 8 when the “no terrestrial coverage” incident gives it an architectural reason to appear.
Landing point / transition

Continue to transfer choices.

07
Activity 7 of 8

Choose under constraints

Transfer the reasoning and institutionalise a practical technology reference map.

8–10 min work · 9–11 min restitutionCore cap · 16 min
Operational trigger to stage

Procurement asks for one communication family everywhere. Students commit one deployment fit, name the decisive assumption, then deliberately flip that assumption and decide whether the choice survives before returning to campus.

Watch for
  • Whether the counterfactual actually changes the decision, and whether students can explain why. Mixed design and legitimate “not enough information” remain acceptable when the missing fact is named.
Validate

Choice must state why the family fits and what dependencies it introduces.

Correct if needed
  • One technology does not have to serve every flow.
  • Unlicensed does not mean infrastructure-free.
Landing point / transition

Close with communication problem → network shape → family → implicit dependencies.

08
Activity 8 of 8

Stress-test and revise

Make the validity domain of Architecture v1 visible through a causal revision.

8–10 min work · 7–8 min restitutionCore cap · 15 min
Operational trigger to stage

Sam brings an incident that falsifies one Architecture v1 assumption. The activity keeps v1 frozen and records one causal v2 move: incident → affected scope → requirement → structural response. No second architecture editor is required.

Watch for
  • Changed assumption → affected scope → requirement under pressure → explicit structural move.
Validate

Robustness is conditional on a failure model and acceptable degraded behaviour.

Correct if needed

Adding components is not automatically robustness; every mitigation has cost and residual risk.

Landing point / transition

End with the reasoning loop, then preview Session 2: assume the path exists and inspect messages, protocol responsibilities, meaning and delivery guarantees.

Reference plate / step further

Revision trace: changed assumption → affected scope → requirement under pressure → design move → residual dependency. NTN appears only for the no-terrestrial-coverage incident; distinguish direct NTN access from satellite backhaul.
